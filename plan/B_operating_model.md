# Appendix B — Operating Model
## Bailey's World / Triangle Senior Dog Barn Sanctuary

*The operating model is the single most important innovation in Bailey's World's design: a **small on-site campus + distributed foster network** with hospice specialization. This appendix details how the system actually runs. Supporting research: `plan/research/06_operating_model.md`, `plan/research/02_peer_sanctuaries.md`.*

---

## 1. Operating Model at a Glance

```
INTAKE SOURCES            →    BAILEY'S WORLD CAMPUS    →    OUTFLOW
(Triangle shelter system)      (20-25 on-site dogs)          (Placement + hospice)
───────────────────            ───────────────────           ───────────────────
• Open-admission shelters  →   Indoor suites                →  Foster network
  (Wake, Durham)               (decompression, medical      →  (25-40 homes)
• Owner surrenders         →    stabilization, quiet        →  Hospice track
  (Senior Safety Net          hospice, controlled transfer   (Forever Loved)
   surrender prevention)       decisions)                   →  Adoption placements
• Veterinary referrals
```

**Scale at steady state:**
- 20–25 max on-site dogs
- 25–40 active foster homes
- 120–220 dogs served annually
- 6.0–6.5 FTE Phase-I staffing (scaling to 10–12 FTE with clinic in Phase III)
- 70–120 active volunteers contributing 36K–60K hours/year

**Why this model vs. a large-campus model:** On-site space is the most expensive capacity to build and operate. Reserving it for decompression, medical stabilization, and the seniors least suited to foster (hospice residents needing 24/7 care) means every dollar of capital pays for what it must. The foster network does the volume work — and compounds into community credibility, adopter pipeline, and donor engagement.

## 2. Foster Network (The Load-Bearing System)

### 2.1 Why Foster Expansion Is the #1 Leverage Move

National context: foster placements have declined since the pandemic peak, and most shelters report recruitment challenges. The **Best Friends "Bring Love Home Challenge" (June 2024)** produced an **11.4% lift in placements** for participating rescues and a sustained **24.6% lift in adoptions through September 2024** (vs. a 1.7% decline at non-participating rescues). Foster expansion is the single highest-leverage intervention for a sanctuary model.

### 2.2 Funnel Benchmarks & Target Sizing

**Funnel assumptions for Bailey's World:**
- Landing-page → application start: 15–25% on optimized pages (2024 M+R Benchmarks)
- Application → approval: 40–60%
- Approval → active foster (first placement within 60 days): 50–70%
- 12-month retention: 60–75% with strong support

**Target pipeline (steady state):**
- 25–40 active foster homes
- 60–100 approved fosters in the standby pool
- **150–250 applications per year** (40–50% approval × 50% activation = 25–40 active)

**Throughput:**
- Healthy-senior track: 2–4 fosters per home per year
- Hospice / forever-foster track: 1 dog per 6–12 months (slower)

### 2.3 Foster Training Curriculum

**Core (adapted from Best Friends + ASPCApro + Muttville):**
- Handling & body language for seniors
- Medication administration
- Mobility aids (ramps, slings, rugs for traction)
- Cognitive-dysfunction handling
- Palliative-care fundamentals
- Hospice boundary-setting (emotional protection for foster)
- Grief support and memorial practices

**Senior-specific layer:** Bailey's World will maintain a written Foster Manual (template from Muttville's January 2019 manual) with NC-specific additions.

### 2.4 Foster Support System

| Support | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Medical reimbursement | 100% of veterinary, diagnostic, therapeutic, emergency + prescriptions |
| Food/supplies | Foster covers unless requests rescue-provided |
| Medical reimbursement SLA | **48 hours** |
| Respite coverage | Back-up fosters on rotation; 1–7 day emergency respite |
| Communications | Weekly check-in text; monthly coordinator call |
| Grief support | Peer-support channel; professional counselor on retainer for loss |
| Recognition | Quarterly events; annual foster-appreciation gala |
| Foster mentor tier | Experienced fosters paid stipend to onboard new ones ("5-Star Foster" model adapted from APA!) |

### 2.5 Hospice / Forever-Foster Program (The Bailey Program)

Given Bailey's story — she was supposed to die earlier and lived six more years — the hospice program should be named with direct reference. **Working name: "Forever Loved" or "The Bailey Program."**

**Structure (adapted from Frosted Faces Molly & Me + Muttville Fospice + OFSDS Geezer Guardians):**

| Element | Bailey's World Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Eligibility | Life expectancy <12 months by vet assessment OR chronic complex condition unlikely to meet standard-adopter appetite |
| Foster stipend | **$150–$300/month** (scaled by care complexity) |
| Medical coverage | 100%, including specialists and emergency |
| Wellness reimbursement | Quarterly $200 reimbursement |
| Bereavement | Paid cremation, memorial care, grief support for family |
| Expected duration | 3–12 months median; outliers 24+ months |
| Donor overlay | "Adopt a Hospice Hero" — named monthly sponsorship matched to named dog; monthly photo/video update to sponsor |

**Capacity:** Reserve **25–35% of foster homes (7–14 homes)** as hospice-capable. This is the mission core.

## 3. Intake Protocol (Capacity-for-Care Framework)

Based on Association of Shelter Veterinarians' 2022 Guidelines for Standards of Care (ASV C4C framework).

### 3.1 Intake Source Hierarchy

1. **Transfer partner** (APS Durham, Wake County Animal Center, Orange County Animal Services, SPCA Wake)
2. **Owner surrender** (after Senior Safety Net counseling — see 3.4)
3. **Veterinary referral**

### 3.2 Capacity-for-Care (C4C) Check — Every Intake

Four simultaneous tests:
1. **Medical capacity** — can we treat what's in front of us?
2. **Behavioral capacity** — can we place this dog?
3. **Housing capacity** — do we have a suite or foster slot?
4. **Financial capacity** — do we have reserve to absorb predictable costs?

**Trigger:** admission may not push on-site or foster census over 90% of cap. If yes, defer or refer to partner rescue.

### 3.3 Medical Triage (at intake)

| Level | Signal | Placement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 Green | Standard senior: dental, arthritis, weight; stable | Foster-eligible within 72 hours |
| 🟡 Yellow | Diagnostic workup or in-progress treatment | On-site suite until stable (avg 7–21 days) |
| 🔴 Red | Terminal or unstable | Hospice track; direct-to-hospice-foster or on-site palliative suite |

### 3.4 Surrender Prevention ("Senior Safety Net" Program)

**2025 ASPCA data (cited by Humane World):** 94% of pet owners considering surrender kept their pet after receiving support. Build a surrender-prevention program covering:

- Subsidized vet care for low-income owners of seniors
- Free food / medication support
- Respite boarding (1–14 days)
- Behavior / mobility coaching

**Strategic value:** High-leverage, low-cost arm; strong grant narrative for Petco Love, Banfield Foundation, Maddie's Fund.

### 3.5 Behavioral Assessment

Use a **modified SAFER assessment adapted for seniors**. Senior dogs often "fail" standard SAFER on food-guarding due to historical resource scarcity — the modified protocol accounts for this.

### 3.6 Transfer Partner MOU Template

Wake County Animal Center's **May 2024 Transfer Partnership Agreement** is the Triangle-area template. Core terms:
- 501(c)(3) required
- Receiving org vaccinates/deworms/microchips/spay-neuters where possible
- Can request but not guarantee specific animals
- Must report adoption outcome
- May not re-transfer without notice

APS Durham operates within the Best Friends Network; its placement-partner process is complementary. **Friends of Wake County Animal Center** sponsors medical-case transfers — a funded channel Bailey's World can tap.

## 4. Dog Flow & Length of Stay

### 4.1 The Math

At 25 on-site max and 120–220 dogs/year, required average on-site LOS is:
- **120 dogs/year → ~76 days** average on-site LOS
- **220 dogs/year → ~41 days** average on-site LOS

### 4.2 Target LOS Structure

| Track | On-site median | Foster median | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Green (healthy senior) | 7–14 days | 60–120 days | Decompression + matchmaking |
| Yellow (diagnostic/workup) | 14–30 days | 90–150 days | Stabilize before foster move |
| Red (hospice) | As long as needed | Open-ended | Off-LOS reporting; survival curves instead |

**Steady-state on-site LOS target: 30–45 days median.** With 25–40 foster homes absorbing 60–80% of dogs within 7–14 days of intake, this is achievable.

### 4.3 Bottleneck Mitigations

| Bottleneck | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Medical complexity extending diagnostics | High-quality intake exam; clear owner-facing summary; standing PO for common senior diagnostics |
| Poor photography/storytelling | Weekly professional photo day; senior-specific video-storytelling format; Pet Diaries format |
| Adoption-fee anxiety | Waive or reduce for senior-to-senior; clear medical-sponsorship messaging |
| Slow home-visit scheduling | Virtual home visits as default; in-person only for flagged cases (reduces time-to-placement 40–60%) |

### 5.2 Phase-III Additions (On-Site Vet Clinic)

| Role | FTE | Comp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medical Director (DVM) | 1.0 | $140K–$155K base + benefits + CE allowance |
| Senior RVT | 1.0 | $50K–$60K |
| Client Services Coordinator | 0.5–1.0 | $42K–$50K |

Employed DVM replaces the Phase-I/II contract DVM model.

### 5.3 Staffing Philosophy — 15-Minute Rule

ASPCA/ASV standard: **15 minutes of direct care per animal per day**. At 25 on-site dogs = 375 minutes/day = 0.8 FTE of direct kennel labor alone. Doubled to 1.5–2.0 FTE when enrichment, medication rounds, and cleaning are layered in. This validates the Phase-I staffing plan above.

### 5.4 Volunteer Labor Multiplier

- Typical: 3–5× staff FTE in volunteer hours
- Bailey's World target: 6.0 FTE paid × 4 multiplier = **~24,000 volunteer hours/year**
- ~70–120 active volunteers at ~8 hours/month
- Independent Sector 2024 NC value of a volunteer hour: $29.95 → **~$1.0M–$1.8M annually in in-kind labor**

## 7. Medical & Care Protocols

### 7.1 Quality-of-Life Assessment (Required Cadence)

**Dual-tool approach:**
- **HHHHHMM (Villalobos) scale** — 7 categories × 0–10 scale; total >35 or all >5 = acceptable QOL
- **Lap of Love Pet QOL Scale** — weekly-trending emphasis

| Status | Cadence |
| --- | --- |
| Intake | Baseline HHHHHMM + Lap of Love score |
| Stable senior | Weekly |
| Declining / hospice | Daily |
| Crisis | At every interaction |

Scores logged in **Shelterluv medical module** — visible to foster, staff, attending vet.

### 7.2 Common Senior Interventions & Budget Assumptions

| Intervention | Cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dental cleaning (intake standard) | ~$700 avg (range $300–$1,500+) | **#1 senior presenting issue**; extractions +$500–$2,500 per tooth |
| Arthritis — Librela (monthly injection) | $85–$250/mo | Breakthrough category; growing use |
| Arthritis — NSAIDs (carprofen, meloxicam, galliprant) | $20–$80/mo | |
| Cognitive dysfunction (selegiline, SAMe, omegas) | $30–$60/mo | |
| Senior bloodwork + urinalysis | $150–$250 per panel | Every 6 months |
| End-stage comorbidity mgmt | $100–$500/mo | Kidney, heart, cancer — variable |

### 7.3 Palliative / Hospice Pathway (Written Protocol)

**Three defined phases:**

| Phase | Trigger | Check-ins | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Comfort care** | QOL >5 all categories | Weekly vet call | Foster + coordinator |
| **Palliative** | QOL 4–5, trending decline | Bi-weekly home visit by vet tech | Foster + Medical Lead + coordinator |
| **Hospice** | QOL <4 any category OR trending down 2+ weeks | Daily vet tech contact | Foster + Medical Lead + ED |

### 7.4 Euthanasia Decision Framework

Written protocol consistent with **AVMA Guidelines** and **Lap of Love clinical standards**. Decision triggers:

- HHHHHMM total <35
- Uncontrolled pain despite multimodal therapy
- Loss of 2+ "core joys" (eating, walking, greeting family)
- Medical emergency with poor prognosis

**Decision requires:** Attending vet sign-off + ED or Medical Lead concurrence + foster consultation when relevant. **Always offer in-home euthanasia via Lap of Love or local mobile vet** rather than clinic. Post-euthanasia: cremation covered, memorial arranged, grief support provided to foster family.

This protocol should be **published publicly** on baileysworld.org — radical transparency builds donor trust and pre-empts social-media crises.

## 8. Adoption Marketing Funnel

### 8.1 Application-to-Placement SLA

- **Application response: 24–48 hours**
- Full processing (references, visit, meet-and-greet): 5–7 days
- Slow processing is the #1 reason warm leads drop off.

### 8.2 Home Visit Policy

**Virtual default** (10-min FaceTime/Zoom walkthrough). In-person only when:
- Renter without landlord documentation
- Flagged references
- Medically complex placement requiring handoff
- Adopter requests in-person

Virtual visits reduce time-to-placement 40–60% in peer rescues.

### 8.3 Digital Storytelling (The Flywheel)

Research (ASPCA Pro, Best Friends "From Kennels to Clicks") shows **video content outperforms static images 3–6×** on engagement and adoption clickthrough.

**Senior-specific formats:**
- "Day in the life" foster vlogs
- HHHHHMM QOL score sheet published publicly (transparency builds trust)
- "This dog has ~X good months left — let's make them the best" honest framing
- Bailey's Story weekly format — each senior gets their own narrative thread
- Before/after medical transformation (dental, mobility, coat)
- Memorial posts for dogs who pass in care (counterintuitively drive huge engagement + donor retention)

### 8.4 Channel Mix

- **Petfinder** (~60% of most rescues' adoption leads; mandatory)
- Adopt-a-Pet
- Rescue Groups
- Owned: Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube
- **Shelterluv auto-syncs to Petfinder + Rescue Groups**

### 8.5 Adoption Fee Structure

| Category | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Standard senior | $150–$350 |
| Senior-to-Senior (adopter 60+) | $50–$75 or waived |
| Hospice / forever-foster | $0; medical covered by Bailey's World |

**Pets for the Elderly Foundation** covers adoption fees for qualified 60+ adopters at participating shelters — apply for partnership.

### 8.6 Post-Adoption Support ("Bailey's Best Benefit")

- **90-day medical grace** (Bailey's World covers any adoption-related medical issue)
- **Lifetime return guarantee** (core to senior-adopter trust)
- Monthly newsletter with behavioral/health tips
- Optional medical stipend for adopters of dogs with known chronic conditions
- 15-day waived co-pays, annual free grooming, 10% retail discount

## 10. Revenue Operations (Social Enterprise Arm)

### 10.1 Public Boarding (Phase II launch)

**Triangle market:**
- Median boarding: **$45/night** in Raleigh (range $26–$59)
- Vet-adjacent boarding (with med administration): $50–$80/night
- Medication surcharge: $15/med at Pupsi; $5–$10/dose bundled at vet-adjacent

**Critical market gap:** many boarders decline dogs 14+ years old. Bailey's World can own the **"senior-safe medicated boarding"** niche.

**Proposed pricing:**
- $65/night standard senior boarding
- +$15/night medicated surcharge (for dogs on 3+ meds or injectable schedules)

**Capacity (Phase II):** 6–10 boarding suites separate from sanctuary suites. 70% utilization target → **$100K–$200K annual revenue at full ramp**.

### 10.2 On-Site Vet Clinic (Phase III)

**Cost-plus hybrid pricing model:**
- **Means-tested** sliding scale for low-income owners: 50–75% of market rate
- **Public market rate** for non-qualifying public clients: generates margin that subsidizes sanctuary medical costs
- **Mission specialty:** geriatric & palliative care differentiates from general-practice vets in the area

**Mission ROI:** Each public-paying client generates gross margin offsetting the cost of caring for 1–2 sanctuary seniors.

### 10.3 Retail

- Branded goods (shirts, stickers, glasses, memorial markers) via Shopify
- Typical rescue retail margin: 30–50%
- Revenue contribution usually <5% of total, but high brand-building value
- **Bailey-branded** line — founder's story as the product narrative

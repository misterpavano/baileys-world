## The Thesis in One Page

**The problem:** Senior dogs (age 7+) are the hardest animals for the Research Triangle's open-admission shelters to place. They stay longer, cost more medically, and adopt out less than one-fourth the rate of younger dogs. North Carolina's statewide shelter euthanasia rate hit **27% in 2024** and is trending the wrong way. Wake County Animal Center was **fined by the state in October 2025** following violations tied to a dog's euthanasia during a sustained capacity crisis. APS of Durham euthanized **891 dogs in 2025 (~35% of dog intake)**.

**The gap:** No senior-dog-specific sanctuary operates a dedicated facility anywhere in Wake, Durham, Orange, or Chatham counties. The closest adjacent programs — Paws4Ever's Legacy Care and Carolina Boxer Rescue's hospice-foster arm — are small and not senior-exclusive.

**The model:** A small on-site campus (20–25 dogs) plus a distributed foster network (25–40 homes), aimed at serving 120–220 senior dogs/year at steady state — **6–10% of the ~1,700–2,200 senior-dog-at-risk SAM in the Triangle region**. The campus is for decompression, medical stabilization, and hospice residents who can't foster. The network handles volume and builds community.

**The ask:** **$1.25M–$3.05M phase-one capitalization**, staged across three gated raises, structured as charitable impact capital (founding gifts, naming rights, DAFs, recoverable grants, PRI/real-estate debt). This is a materially smaller ask than the Triangle's other animal-welfare capital campaigns (SPCA Wake's $27.5M; Wake County Animal Center's $44.6M construction budget) — intentional, because **phase one must be small enough to be believable**.

**The return:** The retirement phase. For every senior dog. At scale. Visibly, in the community.

## 1. Why Bailey's World, Why Now

### 1.1 Bailey

In 2020, Wally was on his third attempt to adopt a dog being transported from Mississippi. Three times the transport failed — the shelter didn't have the transportation capacity, didn't have the volunteer hours, couldn't make it happen. That first-hand experience of shelter-system structural weakness became the origin observation for this entire project.

The adoption agency pivoted Wally toward a dog named Momma Bailey — 7 or 8 years old, being surrendered because her owner was elderly and under financial stress. Wally drove six hours from NYC to Rhode Island to pick her up, learning on arrival that Bailey was also deaf.

Six years followed that neither of them was supposed to have. Bailey and Wally developed their own sign language (she was food-motivated). They walked miles around Brooklyn together. She lived with him through career changes, breakups, personal growth, and a cross-country move to Raleigh. She was "the greatest thing that has ever happened" to him. She died at 15, happy — with the retirement phase the system almost denied her.

**Bailey's World exists to give that to other senior dogs.**

### 1.2 The Structural Tailwind

Three national trends converge on the Triangle:

1. **The Silver Tsunami of senior dogs.** ~28% of America's 89.7 million dogs are now 8+ years old — about 25–30 million senior dogs. The pet-palliative-care market is projected to grow from $1.36B in 2024 to $2.71B by 2033 (CAGR 8.2%). Senior-pet care is no longer a niche — it's mainstream veterinary medicine.

2. **The Triangle's demographic surge.** Raleigh-Cary is one of the 10 fastest-growing metros in the U.S.; the combined Triangle CSA passed 2.28M in 2025 and is heading to 2.5M+ by 2030. Chatham County is 22–25% age 65+ (highest concentration of empty-nesters per capita in the region) — and empty-nesters are the single most aligned senior-dog-adopter profile.

3. **The shelter system's capacity break.** NC statewide euthanasia at 27% and climbing. Wake County Animal Center at 2× capacity "for two years," fined by the state. APS Durham's dog euthanasia rate ~35%. The open-admission shelters structurally cannot prioritize senior dogs' long-term care — they don't have the capacity, the time, or the specialized medical infrastructure.

## 2. The Model

### 2.1 Small Campus + Foster Network

Bailey's World runs as a hub-and-spoke system. The campus is the hub — 12–20 indoor suites in a converted barn, climate-controlled, medically-equipped, quiet — reserved for three specific uses:

1. **Decompression** for newly-intake dogs transitioning out of shelter environments
2. **Medical stabilization** for dogs requiring diagnostic workup before foster placement
3. **Hospice residence** for dogs too fragile or medically complex to place in foster homes

The foster network — 25–40 trained homes at steady state — is the spoke. Fosters take 60–80% of intake within 7–14 days of arrival. Dogs live in real homes; photographs and videos of dogs in foster drive adoption; fosters often become adopters (industry F2A conversion: 25–40%).

This is fundamentally different from both the traditional shelter model (everyone lives at the facility) and pure foster-based rescues (no physical infrastructure). It's the model the pitch deck correctly identified as the investable wedge.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Active foster homes | 25 | 35 | 40 |

| Active volunteers | 70 | 100 | 120 |
| Volunteer hours / year | 25K | 40K | 55K+ |
| Annual operating budget | $875K | $1.4M | $2.2M |

### 2.3 Three Phases, Three Gates

**Phase I — Barn Conversion + Intake.** Phase-one capital builds the core sanctuary. First cohort of senior dogs on-site by Month 18 post-close.

**Phase II — Meet-and-Greet Expansion (Year 2–3).** Public-facing catios, meet-and-greet rooms, adoption-event capacity. Launches public boarding revenue stream (senior-safe medicated boarding — an underserved niche in the Triangle). Incremental capital: $500K–$1M.

**Phase III — On-Site Veterinary Clinic (Year 3–4).** Full NCVMB Facility Permit build-out. Employed Medical Director DVM. Cost-plus public clinic model: means-tested sliding scale for low-income pet owners; market-rate for public clients whose fees subsidize the sanctuary's senior medical cost. Incremental capital: $500K–$1.5M.

**Phase IV — Regional Replication (Year 5+).** The barn-sanctuary thesis scales across NC's agricultural footprint. Bailey's World becomes proof-of-concept for other regions.

### 2.4 Three Audiences Senior Dogs Serve

Per founder narrative — this is the reason the mission works:

1. **Younger families.** Senior dogs teach children about death and grief earlier, more gently than any other life lesson.
2. **People in transition.** Career changes, breakups, personal growth — senior dogs show up unjudgmentally, which Wally learned first-hand with Bailey.
3. **Older adults and households.** Calm, housetrained, trainable, lower-maintenance, deeply bonded.

**You CAN teach an old dog new tricks. You sure can.**

## 7. The Brand: Bailey's World Is Both the Mission and the Moat

The single under-priced lever in the senior-dog-sanctuary category is social media. Old Friends Senior Dog Sanctuary (TN) built a **1.8M Facebook following** over 13 years of consistent daily storytelling — and that following is what lets them run $1–3M/year in planned operating deficits funded from their $21.8M in accumulated assets.

Bailey's World must not under-invest here. A Comms/Content Lead is a Month-3 hire — not a deferred luxury. Content formats:
- Bailey's Story as the hero narrative, retold per senior dog ("This is [Name]'s retirement phase")
- Daily individual-dog posts (OFSDS template — name + backstory + photo)
- Before/after medical transformations (dental, mobility, coat)
- "Day in the life" foster vlogs
- Hospice journeys and memorial posts — these counter-intuitively drive the highest engagement and donor retention
- Live-cam integration on Phase II meet-and-greet space

**Year 1 social targets:** 10K Facebook followers, 5K Instagram, 2.5K TikTok, 100 monthly donors via Bailey's Best Friends program.

## 9. The Staged Raise

Three stages. Five gate-checks between them. No capital closes before its gate.

### Stage 1 — Pre-Development Pledges ($250K–$500K)

**Use:** 501(c)(3) formation + fiscal sponsor + founder runway + parcel diligence (zoning, septic, structural) on top 2 candidates + Grey Muzzle Year-1 application filed.

**Gate 1 (5 green checks to proceed):**
- NC 501(c)(3) filed (IRS 1023 acknowledgment)
- Fiscal sponsor engaged
- Parcel identified with written zoning interpretation
- Septic/well feasibility clean (or engineered-system cost budgeted)
- Barn structural assessment clean

### Stage 2 — Land + Diligence Close ($600K–$1M additional)

**Use:** Parcel close, architectural design, SUP/CUP filing, NCDA&CS Shelter Certificate, NCVMB pre-app letter, first hires (Foster Coordinator, Comms Lead), foster-network seeding.

**Gate 2 (5 green checks to proceed):**
- Land closed under clean title
- SUP/CUP approved (or locked in)
- Conversion plans stamped
- **60–70% of Stage-3 capital committed** (silent phase milestone)
- First 10 fosters committed

### Stage 3 — Conversion + Launch ($1.25M–$3.05M total)

**Use:** Barn conversion construction, medical fit-out, team completion, social-media engine launch, first cohort on-site, 12-month operating runway funded.

**Gate 3 (5 checks to celebrate Phase I complete):**
- Certificate of Occupancy
- First cohort of senior dogs on-site
- Social-media flywheel live (10K+ FB followers)
- 12-month operating runway funded
- Annual Impact Report methodology + dashboard live

## 11. Why This Will Work

Bailey's World is betting on a convergence that's not theoretical — it's already underway:

- **The market gap is real and measurable.** No senior-dog-specific sanctuary operates a facility in the Triangle, despite 1,700–2,200 senior dogs/year at shelter-entry risk.
- **The capital is available.** $11.83B in annual U.S. animal-welfare giving (+7.2% YoY). $240M+ AUM at Triangle Community Foundation. SPCA Wake's 8-figure revenue proves Triangle-scale animal-welfare fundraising is not hypothetical.
- **The precedent exists.** SPCA Wake (22 ac), Paws4Ever (20 ac) validate the footprint and the zoning path in the Triangle. Old Friends SDS, Muttville, Frosted Faces validate the model at scale nationally.
- **The ask is right-sized.** $1.25M–$3.05M sits inside the senior-dog-sanctuary capital campaign modal range (Shep's Place $900K, Home For Good $3M, Pepper's $5M) — materially smaller than Triangle peers' $27.5M–$44.6M asks.
- **The founder is real.** Wally lived the product. Six years with Bailey, from adoption through her passing at 15 — every founding narrative anchor is personally verifiable and emotionally authentic.
- **The model is gated.** Three raises, five checks. No capital commits before its risk is retired.

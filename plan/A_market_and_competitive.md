## 1. Market Thesis

The Research Triangle combines **three conditions that rarely coexist:**

1. **A rapidly growing affluent human population heavily skewed toward educated empty-nesters** — the demographic most likely to adopt senior dogs
2. **A structurally worsening regional shelter euthanasia picture** — NC at 27% and climbing; Wake County Animal Center fined by the state in late 2025; APS Durham euthanizing ~35% of dog intake
3. **A mature philanthropic infrastructure** — $240M+ community foundation AUM, a $9M+ peer animal-welfare nonprofit, and a national animal-giving sector growing +7.2% YoY to $11.83B

Layered on top: the national "Silver Tsunami" — ~28% of the 89.7M U.S. dogs are now 8+ years old, with a $1.36B pet-palliative-care market projected to reach $2.71B by 2033 (CAGR 8.2%).

**Bottom line:** Senior-dog welfare has moved from a niche cause into the mainstream of both veterinary medicine and pet philanthropy. The Triangle is one of the best places in the country to build the flagship.

## 3. The Silver Tsunami — Quantified

### 3.1 US Dog Population Is Aging

- **~28% of US dogs are 8+ years old** (AVMA) — the senior threshold
- Broader industry definition (7+): **~44% of pets are senior**
- Applied to 89.7M US dogs: **~25–30M senior dogs today**
- ~3× the senior population since the 1990s — driven by improved veterinary medicine extending lifespan + spay/neuter reducing juvenile population

### 3.2 Senior-Pet Care Market Growth

- Global pet palliative care: **$1.36B in 2024 → $2.71B by 2033** (CAGR 8.2%)
- Pet hospice specifically: $985M in 2025 → CAGR 11.6% through 2033
- North America ~40% of global palliative spend (~$540M 2024)
- Demand drivers: humanization of pets, Gen X/Boomer aging, expanding pet oncology/cardiology/mobility care

### 3.3 Implication for Bailey's World

The veterinary services ecosystem is rapidly learning to serve geriatric dogs. Bailey's World can position as **both beneficiary of this growth** (partnerships with NC State CVM, private vet clinics) **and thought-leader** for the care model that shelters won't or can't provide.

## 4. Triangle Shelter Crisis (Senior-Specific)

### 4.1 Statewide Context

Per NC Department of Agriculture / WRAL Investigates (November 2025):
- **20,000+ dogs and cats euthanized** in NC shelters in 2024
- Statewide euthanasia rate: **27% in 2024, up from 22% low in 2021** (had been 66% in 2010)
- Dr. Patricia Norris (NC animal welfare director) attributes rising rates to post-pandemic economic pressure and ballooning shelter intake

### 4.2 County-Level Shelter Snapshot

| Shelter | Model | Intake / Outcomes | Senior Posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wake County Animal Center** | Open admission (municipal) | 8,000+ intake; 16% euth 2024; fined by state Oct 2025; "full for two years" | **Prime senior source** — cannot prioritize long-term senior care; Bailey's World is diversion relief |
| **SPCA of Wake County** | Limited admission | 3,652 served 2024; 97.3% lifesaving; $9.33M FY24 revenue | **Cooperative partner** — limited-admission transfers seniors out; highly aligned |
| **APS of Durham** | Open admission (nonprofit-operated municipal contract) | ~7,000 animals/yr; **2,509 dog intake → 891 euthanized in 2025 (~35% dog euth rate)** | **Highest-urgency senior source** in Triangle |
| **Orange County Animal Services** | Municipal | 1,150 dog intake / 82 euth (~7%) | Cooperative; aligned values; referral source for longer-LOS seniors |
| **Chatham County Animal Services** | Open intake | Pre-2024 data; transitioned to Sheriff's Office 2019 | Smaller volume; data gap — direct records request needed |

### 4.3 Senior-Specific Gap

**Nationally:**
- **25% adoption rate for seniors vs. 60% for puppies** (ASPCA)
- **~5% of senior dogs adopted annually**
- Senior & adult dog adoptions **declined 2% and 6%** 2023–2024 while juvenile adoptions grew 6% (Shelter Animals Count)
- Maddie's Fund / Grey Muzzle study: 56% of senior sample was **9+ years old** (the highest-risk cohort)

**Triangle data gap:** No Triangle shelter publicly reports senior-specific intake or outcomes. A defensible business plan should request **age-bucketed intake data directly from Wake/Durham/Orange/Chatham shelters** as a Year-1 action.

## 5. TAM / SAM / SOM

### 5.1 Build-Up

**TAM (Total Addressable Market):** All Triangle dogs aged 7+ at some level of risk for shelter entry or euthanasia annually.

- Triangle dog population: ~435,000
- Senior share (28%): ~122,000 senior dogs
- Shelter-relinquishment rate (~3–5% of pets/yr per ASPCA): **~3,600–6,100 senior dogs at shelter-entry risk per year**

**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** Senior dogs actually entering Triangle shelters that Bailey's World could divert or pull.

| Source | Annual Intake | × Senior Share | × Sanctuary-Appropriate Share | Est. SAM |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wake County Animal Center | ~8,000 | ~30% | ~40% | ~960 |
| APS Durham | ~5,000 | ~30% | ~40% | ~600 |
| Orange County Animal Services | ~1,150 | ~30% | ~30% | <100 |
| Chatham County | estimated | — | — | ~150 |
| Adjacent rural counties (Johnston, Franklin, Wilson, Vance) within 60-mi | — | — | — | +300–500 |
| **Total SAM** | | | | **~1,700–2,200** |

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** Bailey's World steady-state target of **120–220 dogs/year = 6–10% of SAM**.

### 5.2 Why 6–10% Is Defensible

- Concentrates on the medically fragile / long-stay subset that shelters struggle with most — not in competition with faster-placement adoption operations
- Leaves ample headroom for partner rescues to fill other niches
- Matches mature best-in-class senior sanctuary capacity (Old Friends SDS, Grey Muzzle partners)
- Supports a defensible "diversion ROI" narrative: every senior diverted saves the public shelter ~$500–$1,200 in custodial cost

## 8. The Bailey's World Adopter Profile

Based on Petfinder/ASPCA/Maddie's Fund research and Triangle demographics:

**Primary profile (60% of placements):**
- Empty nesters and retirees, age 55–75
- Preference for lower-energy, house-trained companions
- Home ownership, often single-story layout
- Emotional driver: "one last good home" narrative — aligns with Bailey's retirement-phase framing

**Secondary profile (25%):**
- Experienced dog people, age 40–55
- Multiple-dog household historically; comfortable with medical needs
- Often high-income, dog-present-at-office professional class

**Tertiary profile (15%):**
- Families using a senior dog to teach children about grief, death, empathy — this is the narrative Wally specifically cited in Bailey's story
- Caregivers of aging parents / spouses seeking a calm household presence

**Willingness to pay:** In the premium Triangle market, a **$200–$400 senior adoption fee is defensible** if paired with pre-adoption vet work, dental, and a support guarantee. (Peers: Sanctuary for Senior Dogs $125; Forever Loved $100–$200; Old Friends SDS $0; Muttville $250 / waived for hospice; Triangle comps Saving Grace $395, Second Chance $325 — both non-senior-specific.)

Bailey’s World / Triangle Senior Dog Barn Sanctuary

Anchors: 10–20 acre Triangle-area parcel with existing barn structure, under-$1M land cost, zoning path to Special Use Permit for animal sanctuary, on-site housing for founder/staff residence. Target close: Year 1. Supporting research: plan/research/05_site_real_estate.md.

1. Site Selection Thesis

The pitch deck correctly steers away from Wake/RTP-core land pricing and toward Orange/Durham/Chatham. Post-research, the refined ranking is:

County Weighted Score Strategic Posture
Chatham (pre-UDO) 4.05 Best land economics; time-sensitive — UDO effective date was Dec 31, 2025; verify current status before pursuing
Orange 3.90 Safest execution path; Paws4Ever (20 ac) and Orange County Animal Services precedents; strong community receptivity
Durham (rural) 3.45 Best donor proximity; 300-ft residential separation for outdoor animal areas is a design constraint
Wake 2.85 Effectively priced out; retain as TAM, not as site target

Source: plan/research/05_site_real_estate.md, scoring matrix weighted across zoning path (25%), land cost (20%), utilities (15%), barn viability (15%), accessibility (15%), community receptivity (10%).

Primary search area: Central/Western Orange County (Hillsborough outward to Cedar Grove, Efland) AND Northeast Chatham (north of Pittsboro, toward Fearrington and the Orange County line).

Secondary search area: Northern Durham County (Bahama, Rougemont) for donor proximity when a suitable parcel is found with adequate buffer.

2. Triangle Land Market Reality Check (2025–2026)

County Ag/Mid-Tract $/acre Notes
Wake Farmland ~$165K; residential $75K–$200K+ The original research PDF’s $77K/acre figure was residential-median, not farmland; current conditions are materially higher
Durham $84K (5–10 ac parcels) Urban-proximity pricing; thin farmland inventory
Orange $39K median / $47K–$85K avg Wide spread by location — Hillsborough-adjacent vs. western Orange
Chatham $29,997 median Lowest land cost of the four; Chatham Park’s 8,500-ac development is an ongoing upward price pressure through 2030
NC statewide (context) ~$4,170–$5,150 Piedmont premium over statewide is material

Inflation watch: Chatham 2024 home-value appreciation was 12.2%, decelerating to 1.5–2.5% in 2025. Chatham Park is still building; window for below-replacement pricing is closing.

Two structural tax levers that should be assumed in underwriting:

  1. Present-Use Value (PUV) — Forestry path. ≥20 acres + a forest management plan, no income test, reduces assessed value 50–90%. Cleanest structural win if a forested portion of the parcel exists.
  2. Enhanced Voluntary Agricultural District (EVAD). 10-year irrevocable conservation agreement; up to 90% cost-share on Best Management Practices; allows up to 25% gross sales from non-farm products while retaining bona fide farm status.

Critical caveat: NC’s bona fide farm zoning exemption (NCGS 160D-903) does NOT protect sanctuary operations. A working farm can coexist on the same parcel (hay, livestock, timber) and keep that portion tax-advantaged — but the sanctuary use must independently clear county zoning as “kennel,” “animal shelter,” or equivalent.

3. County Deep Dives

3.1 Orange County (Primary — Safest Execution)

Zoning reality - Animal shelter typically requires Special Use Permit (SUP) in most rural zones. - Stated kennel setback: 150 feet from property lines for outdoor runs; fully enclosed indoor housing can offset or eliminate the outdoor setback — key design lever for smaller parcels. - Orange County Animal Services reviews site and building plans; this is a relationship, not just a transaction. - SUP timeline: ~60–120 days (Class A via BOCC or Class B via Board of Adjustment).

Precedents - Paws4Ever operates a 20-acre campus in Mebane (Legacy Care program is the closest existing Triangle analog to senior-specific care, though not senior-exclusive). - Orange County Animal Services has among the lowest euthanasia rates in NC (~7% of intake). - Community receptivity is high in Orange; Paws4Ever and OCAS demonstrate that institutional animal uses are welcome in the county.

Search geography - Hillsborough corridor (US-70, I-85) — closest to RDU and donor base. - Cedar Grove, Efland — cheaper acreage, longer drive time. - Avoid heavy residential density areas (Chapel Hill fringe).

Why it’s the safer pick: Predictable zoning rhythm, existing institutional precedent, strongest community receptivity of the four counties. This is the path a first-time founder should default to if Chatham timing doesn’t work.

3.2 Chatham County (Value Play — Time-Sensitive Diligence Required)

Zoning reality - Critical: UDO effective date was Dec 31, 2025. Current (early-2026) status must be verified via direct call to Chatham Planning at (919) 542-8204 before any commitment. - Under prior regs, kennel/shelter likely requires Conditional Use Permit in R-1/RA zones; rural agricultural districts are more permissive. - NC Senate Bill 382 (December 2024) prohibits local governments from down-zoning without owner written consent — meaning a parcel closed under prior regs enjoys vested-rights protection against subsequent restrictive changes.

Land economics - Median $29,997/acre — by far the lowest of the four counties. - Recent multi-million-dollar deals: $11M for 58 ac near Old US 1 (~$190K/ac); $15M for ~370 ac off Parker Herndon Rd near Briar Chapel (~$40K/ac) — the lower of the two is the planning comp for a rural 10–20 ac parcel.

Deal structure options - Option A — Pre-UDO close: If it’s still possible (verify status), race to close under prior regs with vesting protections. Highest payoff if the UDO tightens kennel standards. - Option B — Post-UDO close: Wait until the UDO is in force so the use is definitive. Safer but likely higher cost as UDO stabilizes the market.

Risk posture - Chatham’s accelerating population (+37% since 2010, >2% annual growth) creates both pricing pressure and rising NIMBY sentiment on the Chatham Park/Briar Chapel fringe. Rural west-county parcels are lower NIMBY risk.

Recommended action: Commission a land-use attorney review of the current UDO status against the sanctuary use before any Chatham offer. This is a $1K–$5K spend that de-risks a $200K–$600K acquisition.

3.3 Durham County (Rural) — Secondary / Donor-Proximity Play

Zoning reality - 300-ft separation required between outdoor animal areas and adjacent residential-use property lines in residential districts. Large-parcel play or heavily-buffered parcel is the design answer. - Northern Durham (Bahama, Rougemont) has larger rural inventory than the urban core. - Animal shelters permitted in IL (Industrial Light) and CG (Commercial General) with use standards — less relevant for a rural-barn footprint but worth knowing.

Why secondary: Best donor proximity (20–30 min from Durham urban core and RDU), but the 300-ft separation requirement and thinner rural inventory make Durham a second-look option. If a parcel ≥15 acres with strong interior buffer appears, it should be pursued.

3.4 Wake County — TAM Only, Not Site

  • Farmland pricing ($165K/acre) is ~4–5× Chatham.
  • SPCA Wake (22 ac) and Saving Grace (7 ac Wake Forest) demonstrate that larger animal-welfare campuses CAN be assembled, but they were acquired at earlier basis costs.
  • Retain Wake as the center of mass for donor, volunteer, and adopter TAM — not as a site candidate.

4. Parcel Screening Checklist (Must-Haves)

Apply to every candidate listing before any site visit:

  • Acreage: 10–20 acres. Under 10 is too tight once setbacks, septic, parking, and outdoor runs are laid out. Over 20 is great if budget allows (PUV forestry path unlocks).
  • Existing structures: Barn or equestrian arena strongly preferred. Ranked by type: (1) former equestrian facility > (2) former boarding kennel > (3) working farm with barn > (4) ag land with permits pulled > (5) raw land.
  • Floodplain: ≤10% of parcel in FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area (Zone AE/A).
  • Utilities: Public water preferred; well must yield ≥10 gpm sustained for a 30–50 dog facility (residential 1–3 gpm is insufficient).
  • Septic: Perc test results in file OR county Environmental Health soil evaluation commissioned before offer. 40–60 dog facility with clinic + residence likely exceeds the 3,000 gpd threshold that triggers engineered system + state DHHS review (6–10 month process).
  • Electrical: 3-phase power at the road. Extension costs $30K–$110K per mile installed in rural NC. Critical for HVAC, kennel-wash, walk-in cooler, medical equipment.
  • Road access: DOT-approved driveway OR adequate frontage for a driveway permit. NCDOT traffic impact analysis may be required for visitor phase.
  • Broadband: Fiber or ≥50 Mbps symmetric. Orange Lumos/ARPA rollout covers ~5,852 homes (completion extended July 2026); Chatham has Brightspeed + AT&T GREAT grants.
  • Setbacks: ≥300 ft available from all property lines (even where county requires less — this builds in noise/odor buffer and future expansion room).
  • Deed/title: Title commitment pulled BEFORE signing. No HOA, no deed restrictions against commercial/institutional/kennel use, no conservation easement that prohibits institutional structures.
  • Drive time: ≤25 min to Chapel Hill OR Downtown Durham — this defines donor, volunteer, and adopter catchment.

5. Red-Flag Traits (Disqualifiers)

  • HOA or recorded deed restrictions against commercial/institutional use

  • 10% FEMA floodplain overlap on the parcel

  • USACE-jurisdictional wetlands (triggers Section 404 permitting)
  • Poor septic soils (engineered systems add $100K+)
  • Neighboring residential density >4 units/acre within 500 ft (NIMBY risk)
  • Former ag-chemical storage barns without a Phase I ESA
  • No direct DOT-approved road frontage
  • Former tobacco-farm use without soil testing
  • Active conservation easements that prohibit institutional structures

6. Barn Conversion Reality Check

Scope Range
Basic barn renovation $30–$200+/sq ft
Animal shelter new construction $350–$400/sq ft typical; national avg $475/sq ft; small shelter can run $550+/sq ft
Medical-grade senior sanctuary conversion (target) $300–$450/sq ft all-in

For a 5,000–8,000 sq ft conversion: hard construction $1.5M–$3.6M, separate from land acquisition. This is the full cost envelope that the Phase-I capital raise must accommodate.

Code pathway: Existing barn as Group U (agricultural) under 2018 NC Building Code Appendix C is exempt from many provisions. Change-of-Use to institutional (I) or business (B) occupancy triggers full commercial code application: structural, egress, ADA, fire protection, commissioned HVAC, plumbing, energy code. Change-of-Use permit runs through County Planning/Inspections — 60–120 days once plans are stamped.

Overall project timeline: 12–24 months closing → certificate of occupancy, assuming clean zoning. Add 6–12 months if SUP is contested.

HVAC non-negotiables for NC humidity + senior dogs: - Oversized system with dehumidification (not just cooling) — NC Piedmont summer dew points run 70–75°F for weeks - Target indoor 68–75°F, 40–55% RH for senior-dog comfort and infection control - Separate zones: medical, intake/isolation, general population - Generator-backed redundancy mandatory for 24/7 medical-fragile population - Budget $15–25/sq ft HVAC alone

8. Phased Site Execution Plan

Stage 1 — Pre-Land Diligence ($250K–$500K committed capital)

  • Form 501(c)(3) (NC incorporation + IRS 1023 + charitable solicitation license)
  • Engage fiscal sponsor for bridge donations during IRS determination (180 days)
  • Commission land-use attorney review of Orange + Chatham zoning against sanctuary use
  • Identify 3–5 candidate parcels across Orange + NE Chatham
  • Septic/well feasibility studies on top 2 candidates
  • Structural engineer assessment of existing barn on top 2 candidates
  • Gate to advance: At least one parcel has clean zoning interpretation, feasible septic, structurally viable barn, clean title

Stage 2 — Land + Diligence Close ($600K–$1M)

  • Close on primary parcel under option or direct purchase
  • File SUP / CUP application with county
  • Neighborhood meeting (required in most counties)
  • Begin architectural design for barn conversion
  • NCDA&CS Animal Shelter Registration (no fee, annual renewal)
  • Gate to advance: SUP granted, conversion plans stamped, building permit in hand

Stage 3 — Conversion + Launch ($1.25M–$3.05M total Phase-I capitalization)

  • Execute barn conversion (12–24 months)
  • Recruit founding staff (Foster Coordinator, Vet Tech Manager, Comms Lead)
  • Establish NC State CVM externship partnership
  • Build foster network ahead of first intake
  • Certificate of Occupancy → first seniors on-site
  • Gate to celebrate: First cohort of senior dogs in residence; social media flywheel live; 12-month operating runway funded

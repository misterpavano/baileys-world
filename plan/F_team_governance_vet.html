1. Founder

Wally Mostafa — Founder & Executive Director

UX leader turned AI builder. Director-level design leadership experience scaling a team from 2 to 30 at Kinesso/IPG. Now building AI tools and advising on AI-product strategy. Currently operating a partner role at Hedgehox and a solo AI consultancy (One Block Away LLC). Award-winning designer (Indigo 2022/2023, Red Dot 2021).

Wally adopted Bailey — a deaf senior dog — in 2020 when a three-times-failed Mississippi transport led him to a Brooklyn surrender. Bailey and Wally spent six years together until her death at 15 in early 2026. The sanctuary is named in her memory.

Why Wally founds and leads this: - Lived the product as an adopter — saw the shelter system’s operational weakness firsthand (the failed Mississippi transports), experienced the senior-adoption journey, and understands the emotional contract the organization has to fulfill - Operational management experience — building and leading cross-functional teams of 30+ in high-stakes, output-focused environments - Narrative and design fluency — essential for the social-media-driven fundraising flywheel that defines the category (OFSDS’s 1.8M Facebook followers is the benchmark moat) - Relocated to the Raleigh Metro area and is fully available for an operating role

Commitment: Full-time Founder + Executive Director role at Bailey’s World from the Phase-I launch. Targeted Year-1 ED compensation: $95K–$110K (below Triangle nonprofit ED comp benchmarks for an org at this stage — Wally is accepting the below-market number as in-kind mission contribution for the first three years).

On-site residency (Phase I, Years 1–3): The founder will reside on-site at the sanctuary during Phase I, providing emergency response, continuous presence for medically fragile and hospice dogs, and around-the-clock institutional knowledge during the operational shakeout period. A dedicated founder’s cottage is deferred to Phase II as part of the expansion capital plan; during Phase I, founder accommodation is integrated into the barn conversion or a transitional structure on the property.

2. Year-1 Hiring Roadmap

The sanctuary is starting solo-founder. Below is the staffing plan that takes Bailey’s World from one person to operational at steady state, with sequencing tied to Phase-I capital release.

2.1 Staffing Ratio Benchmarks (From Peer 990s)

Peer Staff On-Site Dogs Dogs per FTE
Muttville 37 ~70–100 2.7
Old Friends SDS 107 ~120 1.1 (very high-intensity senior/hospice + content operation)
Frosted Faces 14 small + vet hospital 3–5

Industry standard (NACA): 15 min direct care per animal per day = 1,500 min/day for 100 animals. Emergency shelter ideal: 2 staff per 25 animals. Real-world one-FTE-per-3-6-dogs at shelter-grade intensity.

Bailey’s World target at steady state: 8–12 FTE for 20–25 on-site + 25–40 fosters + medical + admin + content + development.

2.2 Hiring Sequence

Clinical staffing precedes all non-clinical hires. The Vet Tech Manager is the first hire after the founder, enabling safe intake from Month 2 onward and closing the medical-response gap that would otherwise exist during the first half-year of operations. This is the correct sequencing for any senior-dog sanctuary model that accepts hospice and medically fragile intakes — which is the stated model here.

2.3 Around-the-Clock Care Model

Bailey’s World operates a hybrid 24/7 coverage model designed for the specific needs of a 20–25 dog senior sanctuary. The model has three layers.

Day coverage (6am–10pm). Full staff presence. Dogs spend the majority of the day in the center Living Room — a glass-enclosed social space — or in the adjoining outdoor yard. Visiting hours and adopter meet-and-greets run during this window. The RVT, Animal Care Lead, and Foster Coordinator are on-site during core hours.

Night coverage (10pm–6am). Dogs return to individual suites in the left wing of the barn, each with a dedicated sleeping area, climate control, and a window. A paid Overnight Care Associate is on-site from Month 9 onward, when the on-site population exceeds 15 dogs. Before Month 9 and during transitions, the resident founder provides overnight coverage supported by a monitored camera system and a 5-minute-response on-call protocol with the RVT.

Founder residency (Phase I). The founder resides on-site during Phase I (Years 1–3), providing emergency response, continuous presence for medically fragile and hospice dogs, and around-the-clock institutional knowledge during the operational shakeout period. A dedicated founder’s cottage is deferred to Phase II capital planning; during Phase I, founder accommodation is integrated into the barn conversion or a transitional structure on the property.

The Living Room is explicitly a day-use space, not a 24/7 shared sleeping environment. This design choice is both clinical (senior dogs need private, quiet sleep spaces to manage stress, monitor intake, and support medical oversight) and regulatory (indoor-only housing with individual suites waives the 150-ft kennel setback in Orange County and strengthens the position in Durham and Chatham).

2.4 Volunteer Engine

Foster peer benchmarks show volunteer-FTE multipliers of 3–5× paid staff. Bailey’s World Year-1 target: 100 active volunteers (across fostering, socialization, transport, photography, events, grooming, administrative). Volunteer coordinator (role 6 above) manages the pipeline.

Volunteer management software: Better Impact, VolunteerMatch, or Galaxy Digital (~$1K–$3K/yr).

4. NC State College of Veterinary Medicine Partnership

4.1 Why This Matters

NC State CVM is ranked #3 nationally, treats 30,000+ animals/year, and graduates ~100 DVMs/year (~80 NC residents). It is the single most strategic institutional partnership in the Triangle for Bailey’s World — providing:

  1. Clinical-year externship pipeline — student labor + future-hire funnel
  2. Shelter Medicine rotation partnership — credentializes the medical program
  3. Research collaboration — geriatric welfare, palliative care protocols
  4. Alumni donor pipeline — ~3,000 NC-based DVM alumni
  5. Credibility with investors and Grey Muzzle / Maddie’s Fund / ASPCA grant committees

4.2 Program Specifics

  • Clinical-year externships: Senior DVM students must complete extramural rotations. Minimum 10 full working days (80 hours) per 2-week rotation. Visiting DVM student externships: 2–4 weeks minimum.
  • Community Practice and Shelter Medicine rotation: Elective track through the Terry Companion Animal Veterinary Medical Center. Marketed as “well-suited for students seeking careers in shelter medicine.”
  • Flag — data gap: NC State does NOT host a dedicated Maddie’s Shelter Medicine Program (Cornell, UW-Madison, UC Davis, Florida do). Shelter medicine at NC State is an elective, not a residency/fellowship track. Bailey’s World could be part of the case for expanding this.

5. Veterinary Staffing Plan

5.1 Phase I–II (Pre-Clinic): Relief/Contract DVM Model

  • ~20 hours/week on-site veterinary coverage
  • $100–$120/hr via Roo (largest platform, Raleigh avg $118/hr), direct-contract relationships, or Holiday Vet Relief (NC/VA specialist)
  • Annual cost: ~$110K–$125K
  • Complemented by: Registered Veterinary Technician (RVT) Manager (employee) + 1–2 vet assistants

RVT scope under NC regs (key points): - Collect blood/specimens - Run parasite and heartworm tests, lab tests - Take radiographs - Perform dental cleaning/polishing - Administer vaccines including rabies (under DVM supervision)

An experienced RVT can absorb 60–70% of routine sanctuary medical tasks. This is the single largest lever for Phase-I–II medical economics.

5.2 Phase III (Clinic Launch): Employed DVM

  • Full-time Medical Director DVM
  • Target comp: $140K–$155K base + benefits + CE allowance (Triangle avg is $146K; sanctuary matches to compete with private practice)
  • PSLF (Public Service Loan Forgiveness) eligibility is a material recruitment benefit for DVMs with student debt

5.3 Recruitment Strategy

  • NCVMA Career Center (200+ NC DVM postings at any time — sustained double-digit vacancy)
  • NC State CVM graduating class pipeline (Year-3+ after externship partnership establishes)
  • Direct outreach to shelter-medicine specialists (very small US pool — individual recruitment)
  • Relief-network relationship-building (try before you hire)

5.4 Telehealth Supplement

NC requires in-person VCPR (Veterinarian-Client-Patient Relationship) before telehealth delivery. Within that constraint, platforms like Vetster, Airvet, Dutch.com can supplement for: - After-hours triage for foster families - Medication adjustment follow-ups - Adopter post-placement support

Budget: $3K–$8K/yr for a telehealth layer.

See Appendix C for full site-strategy detail. 6.3 Rabies Control (NC G.S. 130A-185)

  • Dogs/cats must have current rabies vaccine by age 12 weeks (latest 16)
  • Adopted animals vaccinated within 30 days of adoption
  • Administered by licensed DVM, licensed RVT (at shelter), or Certified Rabies Vaccinator (CRV)
  • Action: certify 2–3 staff as CRV for cost-efficient intake vaccination
